● Tutor recommendations ● Student progress ● An upcoming lesson ● Tutor profiles ● Homework or goals
The visual should demonstrate that Studdy supports the full tutoring relationship rather than only tutor search. 5. Suggested Homepage Structure
Section 1: Hero
The opening section should communicate:
● Trusted tutor discovery ● Student progress visibility ● Tutor-first tools ● Primary and secondary calls to action
Section 2: How finding a tutor works
Suggested steps:

1. Tell Studdy what support is needed. 2. Review recommended tutors. 3. Compare profiles, pricing and suitability. 4. Request a booking. 5. The tutor accepts or declines. 6. If declined, Studdy suggests suitable alternatives. 7. Begin tutoring and track progress.
   Section 3: Why families can trust Studdy
   This section should include:
   ● Tutors are interviewed and approved. ● Identity and claimed qualifications are verified where applicable. ● Profiles show ratings, experience and lesson history. ● Pricing is visible. ● Progress, homework and lesson summaries remain organised. ● Booking requests require tutor acceptance by default. ● Parents can report concerns and receive support.
   Section 4: Tutor discovery preview
   The homepage should show approved tutor profiles where available.
   Tutor cards may include:
   ● Profile photo ● First name ● Subjects

● Year levels ● Online or in-person format ● Starting price ● General availability status ● Overall rating ● Verification indicators ● Lesson count ● Response time
Where there are not enough real tutor profiles, Studdy may show clearly labelled example profiles.
Example profiles must never be presented as real tutors.
Section 5: Student progress
This section should show that Studdy provides more than booking.

● Simple parent progress overview ● Detailed progress by skill or curriculum area ● Goals ● Concerns ● Homework ● Upcoming tests and important dates ● Approved lesson summaries ● Assessments and learning recommendations
Section 6: Tutor operating tools

Join the tutor network 6. Find a Tutor Journey
Selecting Find a Tutor should begin a short matching questionnaire.
The first version should remain short enough to complete without progress saving.
If the questionnaire becomes longer later, signed-in users should be able to save and resume.
The questionnaire should support multiple subjects for one student.
Suggested information
● Student name ● Subject or subjects ● Year level ● Main learning need ● Online or in-person preference ● Preferred days and times ● Budget ● Upcoming tests or deadlines ● Desired lesson frequency

<!-- page 6 -->

● Tutor-type preferences ● Teaching-style preferences ● Introductory-meeting preference
The student’s name should be requested after the first few useful matching questions rather than as the first screen. 7. Learning Needs
Parents should be able to select needs such as:
● Building confidence ● Catching up ● Extension ● Exam preparation ● Homework support ● Assessment preparation ● Study habits ● Subject-specific difficulties ● Ongoing general support
Parents should also be able to provide free-text context. 8. Parent Matching Priorities
Parents should be able to rank what matters most.
Possible factors:
● Availability ● Price ● Experience ● Rating ● Teaching style ● Subject specialisation ● Location ● Online or in-person format ● Response time
These preferences should influence recommendations without guaranteeing a perfect match.
Parents should be able to change filters after seeing results. 9. Tutor-Type and Format Preferences
Parents may choose to include or exclude:
● Secondary-school student tutors ● University student tutors ● Experienced independent tutors ● Qualified teachers ● Subject specialists ● Online-only tutors

<!-- page 7 -->

● In-person-only tutors ● Tutors outside a preferred price range ● Tutors without experience at the student’s year level
Parents may also express optional preferences relating to:
● Tutor age range ● Tutor gender ● Cultural or language background
These should be handled as optional matching preferences rather than quality-ranking factors. 10. Teaching Style
Tutors may describe their teaching style using optional labels such as:
● Structured ● Patient ● Encouraging ● Energetic ● Practical ● Exam-focused ● Flexible ● Direct
Tutors may also include an optional section:
What a lesson with me is like
This section may describe:
● Typical lesson structure ● Teaching methods ● Use of resources ● Homework approach ● Communication style ● Student participation 11. Tutor Recommendation Results
Studdy should show a ranked shortlist of several tutors.
It should avoid presenting one tutor as the unquestionable best match.
Each recommended tutor may show simple reasons such as:
● Matches your preferred times ● Highly rated for the selected subject ● Within your price range ● Experienced with exam preparation ● Offers your preferred lesson format ● New tutor with strong availability
The recommendation explanation should remain understandable and lightweight.

<!-- page 8 -->

Studdy should avoid exposing detailed ranking logic. 12. New Tutor Fairness
Recommendations should deliberately include suitable new tutors.
New tutors should not be excluded simply because they have fewer ratings or completed lessons.
Possible mechanisms include:
● New-tutor visibility boost ● Rotating exposure ● Suitability-first ranking ● Separate “New to Studdy” label ● Availability-based opportunities
New tutor exposure should never override fundamental suitability or parent preferences. 13. Public Tutor Profile Access
Visitors may browse tutor profiles before signing in.
Public information may include:
● Profile photo ● First name ● Subjects ● Year levels ● Teaching approach ● Starting price ● General availability status ● Overall rating ● Verification indicators ● Completed lesson count ● Introductory video ● “What a lesson with me is like”
Exact availability should require sign-in.
Suggested availability labels include:
● Available this week ● Accepting new students ● Limited availability ● Existing students only ● Waiting list available
Studdy may later prompt visitors to sign in to view:
● Exact days and times ● Full written reviews ● Detailed subject ratings ● Booking options ● Favourites

<!-- page 9 -->

● Waiting-list access
The site should provide enough value before requiring sign-in. 14. Introductory Videos
Tutors may optionally upload a short introductory video.
The video should help parents understand:
● Communication style ● Personality ● Teaching approach ● Professionalism
Studdy should require:
● Admin moderation ● Captions or a transcript ● A written alternative ● Appropriate privacy and content standards 15. Favourites
Parents may save tutors to a family-wide favourites list.
Favourites may be filtered by:
● Child ● Subject ● Year level ● Online or in-person format ● Availability status
Parents may add private notes to favourited tutors.
Example notes:
● Strong fit for maths ● Maya liked the intro video ● Available after school ● Consider for exam preparation
Tutor profiles may show an aggregated signal such as:
12 families have saved this tutor
Tutors should not see which specific families have favourited them before a booking request or authorised message exists.
Whether tutors see aggregate favourite counts remains optional and admin-configurable. 16. Favourite Availability Alerts
Parents may opt into alerts when a favourited tutor:

<!-- page 10 -->

● Begins accepting new students ● Opens suitable availability ● Adds a relevant subject ● Offers a new assessment or package
Email should be the initial channel.
Push notifications may be added when Studdy becomes an app or progressive web application.
Parents should control these alerts through notification settings. 17. Messaging
Pre-booking messaging should be added later when Studdy has reliable in-platform messaging and application notifications.
Until then, parents may:
● Request an introductory meeting ● Submit relevant information with a booking request ● Review detailed tutor profiles ● Receive tutor responses through the booking flow
No private communication between a dependent student and tutor should be hidden from the parent. 18. Navigation Direction
Suggested public navigation:
● Find a Tutor ● How It Works ● For Tutors ● Resources ● Assessments ● About Studdy ● Sign In
Primary navigation button:
Find a Tutor
Secondary navigation action:
Join as a Tutor
The navigation should remain simple and avoid exposing every future feature before it is available. 19. Public Website Content Management
Routine public-site content should be configurable through the admin interface where practical.

<!-- page 11 -->

Admin-editable areas should eventually include:
● Homepage headings ● Supporting copy ● Calls to action ● Pricing statement ● Featured tutors ● Tutor-profile order ● Trust indicators ● Homepage announcements ● Example content ● Resource and assessment features ● Frequently asked questions ● Footer links ● Notification banners
Major layout and structural changes may still require code. 20. Redesign Principles
Show value before requiring sign-in
Visitors should understand the platform and browse useful tutor information before creating an account.
Explain the tutoring relationship
The website should show the journey from tutor discovery through lessons, homework and progress.
Build trust through evidence
Tutor profiles should use verification, experience, ratings and transparent pricing.
Keep discovery enjoyable
The matching questionnaire should feel quick, personalised and useful.
Preserve parent choice
Studdy recommends several tutors and allows parents to browse beyond the shortlist.
Give tutors visible importance
Even with a parent-first call to action, the website should clearly present Studdy as a serious tutor operating platform.
Reflect the mature product direction
The redesign should prepare for assessments, resources, progress tracking, payments and multi-tutor growth.
Keep future features honest

<!-- page 12 -->

Features that are planned but not yet available should be clearly identified or omitted from live claims.
